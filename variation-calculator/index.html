A variation becomes a write-off in two steps, not one: it gets agreed without a written instruction, and then at final account you cannot evidence it well enough to hold it. Most contractors put the first number higher than they expect to and the second lower. Both are worth arguing about honestly.

Why a variation
becomes a write-off.

Nothing here is a process failure. It is what happens when a programme moves faster than paperwork, which is every programme.

One

The programme will not wait

Something has to change and it has to change today. It gets agreed on site between two people who both mean it. Raising it properly takes a day the job does not have, so it is done and the paperwork is left for later.

Two

Later never arrives

The next thing happens. The instruction stays in a phone call, a WhatsApp message, or a photograph of a marked-up drawing on somebody's camera roll. It is not lost, exactly. It is just not anywhere a second person would look.

Three

Final account

Months later, two reasonable parties remember it differently, and the one with the better record wins. Usually neither has one, so it gets split — and splitting it is the write-off. The relationship survives it. The margin does not.

What a record
has to have.

Four things, and the fourth is the one that decides whether it is worth anything at final account.

Who

A name, not a company

"The client agreed" is not a record. A person agreed, and the record has to say which one.

When

The date it was agreed

Not the date it was written up. Those are different dates and the gap between them is what gets attacked.

What

The price at that moment

A change agreed without a number attached is an argument scheduled for later.

And

That nobody can edit it afterwards

A record either side could have changed after the fact proves nothing. This is the part a spreadsheet and an email thread cannot do, and it is the whole difference.
