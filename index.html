The part that matters

Why "I never approved that"
stops working.

Anyone can promise a record. These are the three things that make it hold up months later, when the job is finished and the argument is about who said what.

The log refuses to be edited

Every action is written append-only and hash-chained: each record's hash covers its content and links to the one before it. The database rejects updates and deletes outright, and an edit made around it breaks verification from that point onward. There is a button that checks the whole chain.

A signature captures the document, not a tick

Signing a contract records the name, the moment, the IP address it came from, and a hash of that exact version of the document. Change a word afterwards and the hash no longer matches what was signed — which is the difference between a signature and a checkbox.

Approvals supersede. They never overwrite

When something is re-agreed, the new approval points back at the one it replaces and both stay. So the question at final account isn't "what does the system say now" — it's "show me the sequence", and the sequence is there with a name and a price against each step.
